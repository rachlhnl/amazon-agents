Search visibility (Brand Analytics, top 1,500 queries)

Dagacci's share at each funnel step

How to read this
Across the top 1,500 scrub searches, each bar is Dagacci's share of that step. Impressions: how often a Dagacci item appeared in results. Clicks / cart adds / purchases: Dagacci's share of each action. If bars grow from top to bottom, shoppers prefer Dagacci once they see it, and the fix is more visibility. If they shrink, shoppers see it but choose others, and the fix is the listing (price, images, reviews, fit).

Seen vs. bought, by shopper segment

Impression share vs purchase share, 3-week pool. A wide gap to the right means shoppers buy when they see Dagacci.

Impression sharePurchase share
How to read this
Searches are grouped by who they're for. The blue dot is how often Dagacci is seen; the orange dot is how often it's bought. Orange far to the right of blue means the segment converts very well and is under-served: increase ads and keywords there. Orange left of blue would mean wasted visibility.

Search query opportunities

Under-exposed purchase share ≥ 1.5× impression share: more ad or SEO visibility should pay off. Leaking seen often but rarely bought: check price, images or fit for that query. Top 60 non-branded queries by volume.

How to read this
One row per search phrase (3-week average). Impr. share is how often Dagacci was shown, and Purch. share how often it won the sale. Under-exposed: it wins more sales than its visibility, so raise bids or add the phrase to listings. Leaking: it is shown but loses, so check which listing appears for that phrase. Rows with near-zero share and high volume (caps, pants, competitor names) are gaps: demand Dagacci isn't competing for. The price column compares the median price shoppers paid overall with Dagacci's.
Fit, returns & listing health (Voice of the Customer, new-condition SKUs)

Fit complaints by size

Orders where the top bad-experience (NCX) reason is a fit complaint, per 100 orders (sizes with 300+ orders)

Too smallToo large
How to read this
For each size: of every 100 orders, how many had a bad-experience report whose main reason was too large (red, right) or too small (blue, left). If one side dominates at every size, the sizing itself is off, not individual units. Red dominating means the products run large; the fix is the size chart and a 'size down' note. Track the red bars shrinking after the fix.

Why customers had a bad experience

Estimated orders with a bad-experience (NCX) report, by top reason

Return badge exposure

Amazon shows a "frequently returned" badge on high-return listings, which can lower conversion

How to read this
Bad-experience (NCX) orders are orders with a return, complaint or negative feedback, grouped by the main reason Amazon recorded. Amazon reports only the top reason per listing, so these counts are estimates. The return badge bar shows the share of orders on listings that display the 'frequently returned' warning (red) or are close to it (orange). Shrinking the orange is the urgent goal: it's the share you can still protect.

Share of customers who are new to brand

% of the week's customers buying Dagacci for the first time in 12 months

Repeat purchase rate

% of the week's customers who bought Dagacci before

How to read this
New to brand is the share of the week's customers who had not bought Dagacci in the past 12 months. High (~70%) means Dagacci is mostly a first purchase. Repeat purchase rate is the share who had bought before. If it falls while new-to-brand stays high, customers try Dagacci once and don't come back.

Market Basket: weighted share of the top-3 co-purchased items

How to read this
Amazon's Market Basket report lists the top 3 items bought in the same order as each Dagacci item. Another Dagacci item: customers buying several colors or sizes, which supports bundles. Non-scrub item: what else this shopper needs (stethoscopes, study guides), which suggests kits and cross-promotion. Competitor scrubs: shoppers hedging between brands, usually over fit.

Competitor scrubs bought in the same order

How to read this
Competitor scrubs that appear in the same order as a Dagacci item, weighted by order volume. These brands are Dagacci's true head-to-head rivals on Amazon. Benchmark their size charts and prices, and target their product pages with display ads.
